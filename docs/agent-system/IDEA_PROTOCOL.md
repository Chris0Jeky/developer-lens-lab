# Idea and candidate protocol

Agent-generated ideas are welcome, labelled as agent-generated, and cheap to capture — and no
idea reaches the execution wave without independent criticism. Three capture modes: a low-cost
note/issue; a structured proposal with evidence and trade-offs; or a bounded exploratory
branch/worktree when quick experimentation cheaply resolves uncertainty.

## Lifecycle

`INBOX → DEDUPLICATED → QUESTION → CHEAP_EVIDENCE → PROPOSAL → CRITIC → PREREGISTRATION →
READY → ACTIVE → DECIDED → ARCHIVED`

The critic gate is mandatory before promotion to the wave; the critic may reject an idea
without implementation, and a rejected idea is archived with why (failure archive when work was
attempted, issue closure otherwise).

## Required idea record (for PROPOSAL and beyond)

Research/product question; target subject/unit (system, team, individual, person-resolvable
grouping — explicit, per the layered subject policy); why it matters; construct definition;
baseline; candidate; data requirements and lane; privacy/secret implications; synthetic test;
own-data test; curated-public test; split/holdout plan; primary metric; guardrails;
uncertainty; confounds; failure/abstention behaviour; cost; the cheapest discriminating
experiment; product presentation opportunity; critic verdict.

## Critic responsibilities

Challenge: person-shaped or misleading unit choice; data leakage; unfair baselines; unmeasured
confounds; non-identifiability; duplication of an existing method; poor product value;
irreversible data burden; results that cannot be explained visually; and experiments
disproportionate to their question. New candidates enter the registry only when they test a
materially different assumption — never merely to enlarge a tournament.
